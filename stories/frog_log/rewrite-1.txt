A tiny toad rested by the marsh at sunrise. The water was flat and gray, with a light fog floating among the grasses. A songbird started up in the trees nearby, and the toad lifted its chin to listen, its throat beating slowly.

Lying half in the water at the shallow end was a fallen tree trunk, black from the rain and furry with green growth. The toad had gone past it every morning all summer. This morning the first sunlight was just reaching the other shore, so the toad tucked its hind legs under its body and hopped up onto the trunk, touching down so gently that the green growth hardly moved.

Up there it could see everything: the floating leaves, the soft muddy slope where the cattle came to drink, and the bright strip of sky above the hills. When the sun climbed over the ridge, the toad swelled its throat and called out to the morning light, a low, scratchy sound that rolled over the water. Another toad replied from across the marsh, then a third.

It stayed on the trunk a little while, letting the warmth soak into its back. Then something stirred in the grasses, and its old wariness came back. It sprang into the shallows in one long arc, splashed down, and paddled off under the floating leaves. Only a circle of ripples remained, and these faded until the marsh lay calm again.
