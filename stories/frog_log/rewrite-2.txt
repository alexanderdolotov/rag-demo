At first light a solitary green amphibian positioned itself on the margin of the freshwater basin. Surface conditions were calm, with low vapour above the emergent vegetation. An avian call originated from the tree line, and the specimen oriented its head accordingly while its vocal sac pulsed at a slow rate.

A waterlogged length of driftwood, darkened by moisture and colonised by moss and lichen, lay partially immersed in the shallow zone. The amphibian had traversed this location daily throughout the season. As solar radiation reached the opposite bank, it flexed its hind limbs and bounded onto the damp timber with minimal disturbance to the moss cover.

From this elevated position the full basin was visible: floating vegetation, the sloping mudflat used by livestock for drinking, and a brightening band of sky over the hills. Once the sun rose above the ridge, the animal inflated its vocal sac and vocalized toward the sunrise, a coarse, low-frequency call audible across the water. A second individual responded from the far side, followed by a third.

The animal remained in place for several minutes, absorbing warmth. Movement near the reeds then triggered a defensive response. It dived from the timber in a single extended arc, entered the water with a modest splash and withdrew beneath the lily pads. Concentric ripples dissipated gradually and the surface returned to stillness.
