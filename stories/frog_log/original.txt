A small green frog sat beside the pond at dawn. The water was still and grey, and a thin mist drifted between the reeds. Somewhere behind the willows a bird began to sing, and the frog turned its head toward the sound, its throat pulsing slowly.

Half submerged in the shallows lay an old mossy log, dark with rain and soft with years of lichen. The frog had passed it every morning of the summer. Today the first light was touching the far bank, and the frog gathered its back legs beneath it. With one strong push it jumped onto the log, landing so lightly that the moss barely shivered.

From the top of the log it could see the whole pond: the lily pads, the muddy slope where the cows came to drink, the pale ribbon of sky growing brighter above the hills. As the sun finally cleared the ridge, the frog puffed out its throat and croaked, a deep, rough call that carried across the water. A second frog answered from the far side, and then a third.

For a few minutes the frog stayed where it was, warming its green back in the new light. Then a ripple spread near the reeds, and it felt the old caution rise. It leaped off the log in a long curve, hit the water with a small splash, and swam away beneath the lily pads, leaving only a ring of ripples that slowly smoothed out until the pond was still again.
