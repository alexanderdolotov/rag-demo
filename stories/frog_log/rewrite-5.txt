By full daylight the only trace left of the small green creature was a set of fading rings on the surface of the pond. It had spent the first light of the day perched on a moss-covered length of timber, singing to the sunrise, before plunging back into the water and disappearing.

The sequence began in the grey hour before dawn, when the creature settled near the reeds as mist drifted over the surface and a bird called from the willows. It vaulted onto the half-sunken timber, an old soft piece of wood it had used all summer, and sat there as the light reached the far bank.

From that perch it surveyed the lily pads, the muddy slope where cattle drink and the paling sky. As the sun cleared the hills it filled its throat and sent out a rough, carrying cry; two others replied from the opposite side. It lingered to soak up the warmth. A disturbance in the reeds then ended the visit. The creature bounded away in a long arc, dived in with a small splash and vanished among the lily pads.
