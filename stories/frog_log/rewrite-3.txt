I was sitting by the water at daybreak, small and green, and I remember how quiet the pond was. A low mist hung over the reeds and somewhere back in the willows a bird began to sing. I turned toward it and felt my throat flutter the way it does when I am calm.

Out in the shallows there is an old fallen trunk, soft with moss, and I had hopped up onto it nearly every morning that summer. That day the light was just touching the far side. I pulled my back legs in underneath me and sprang, and I landed so quietly that the moss never even moved.

From up there you can see everything. The lily pads, the muddy slope where the cows come down to drink, the sky going pale above the hills. When the sun cleared the ridge I puffed myself up and sang as loudly as I could, and another voice answered me from across the water, then another.

I stayed a while, just warming my back. Then I saw a ripple by the reeds and my nerves came back, the way they always do. I leapt off in a big curve, splashed in and swam under the lily pads. When I looked back the water was smooth again, as if I had never been there.
