The autumn harvest festival at Maren Hollow constitutes an annual ritual cycle transmitted across generations. It is held on the first Saturday following completion of the cereal harvest. By midday the central square is decorated with braided straw and dried fruit, and trestle tables are transported from the mill.

The principal ceremony is a lantern procession. At dusk, children assemble at the churchyard entrance with paper lanterns, which the community's eldest woman ignites from a single flame carried from her domestic hearth. Participants proceed silently to the orchard, where the lanterns are suspended from branches as an expression of gratitude for the season's yield.

A second observance concerns the ceremonial loaf. Prepared in the form of a wheat sheaf, it may not be divided until the miller's youngest child has broken the first portion manually. Distribution is universal: each household, and any itinerant visitor, receives a share, and declining is held to portend adversity during winter. The festival concludes with circle dancing around a bonfire and a final rite in which participants cast grain into the flames and state a wish. Elders maintain that wishes are fulfilled only if never repeated.
