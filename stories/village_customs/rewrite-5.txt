By midnight the square at Maren Hollow was silent, lanterns still shining in the orchard, after a festival the villagers say has not changed in generations.

It had started that morning, the first Saturday after the final field was reaped, when the square was hung with straw braids and dried apples. At dusk the children took paper lanterns to the church gate, where the eldest woman lit each from one candle taken from her hearth, and the group walked wordlessly to the orchard to hang them as thanks for the fruit.

Next came the ritual of the harvest bread, shaped like wheat. Only after the miller's youngest child tore off the first piece could it be cut, and every household and passing stranger received a share; refusing one was said to bring a hard winter. The night ended with dancing around a bonfire and each person throwing grain into the flames with a private wish, which elders say fails if repeated.
