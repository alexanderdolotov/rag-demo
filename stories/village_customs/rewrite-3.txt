When I was little, the harvest festival in Maren Hollow was the best night of the whole year. We did everything the way my grandmother said her grandmother did it, and nobody dared skip a step. It always came the first Saturday after the last field was cut.

By lunchtime the square was covered in plaited straw and strings of dried apples. At dusk we children stood by the church gate with our paper lanterns, and the oldest woman in the village lit every one of them from the same candle, which she had carried from her own stove. Then we walked up to the orchard without a word and hung the lanterns in the branches to say thank you to the trees.

Then came the loaf, made to look like a bundle of wheat. You could not cut it until the miller's smallest child tore the first bit off, and everyone got a piece, even people who were just passing. If you said no, they told you that you would have a terrible winter. At the end we all danced round the bonfire and threw a handful of grain on it and made a wish. Grandmother said it only worked if you never told.
