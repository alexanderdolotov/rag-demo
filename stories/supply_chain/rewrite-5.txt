Oakridge Furnishings earned 18 percent less this quarter, $96 million, because getting its overseas sofas, tables and lamps onto store shelves took far longer and cost far more than usual.

Overcrowded ports kept containers waiting an average of eleven days to be unloaded, up from three, and the price of shipping a container more than doubled, pushing transport to 14 percent of sales from 9. Revenue still grew 3 percent to $1.4 billion, but the proportion kept after product costs fell to 33.1 percent from 35.2 percent, which the chief financial officer put entirely down to shipping and storage. About one store in ten ran short of stock during back-to-school.

The company has chartered more ships, moved some volume to a less crowded west coast port and started building inventory earlier. Conditions are expected to stay hard until at least mid-next year; the sales forecast and dividend were not changed.
