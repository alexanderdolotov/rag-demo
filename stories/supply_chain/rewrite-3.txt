For about two months the back of my store has been half empty. The sofas we ordered in the spring are still sitting on ships outside the harbour, and on the days when they do get in, the trucks sit waiting for hours. Corporate says the containers wait eleven days to be unloaded now, and it used to be three.

On Wednesday the company told investors why profits are down. Sales rose three percent to 1.4 billion, but profit fell 18 percent to 96 million, because sending a container across the ocean costs more than twice what it did a year ago. Transport is now 14 percent of sales, up from 9, and the margin lost about two points.

They have hired more ships, they are using a quieter port on the west coast, and we are being asked to order earlier in the year. Nobody expects it to get better before next summer. I just hope the shelves are full for back-to-school, because about one store in ten ran out during it this year.
