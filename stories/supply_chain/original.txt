Oakridge Furnishings said on Wednesday that congestion at major ports and soaring ocean freight rates reduced its profit in the third quarter. Net sales were $1.4 billion, up 3 percent, but net income fell 18 percent to $96 million. The company, which imports most of its sofas, tables and lighting from factories overseas, said containers sat for an average of eleven days waiting to be unloaded, compared with three days a year ago.

Freight costs per container more than doubled over the past year, and Oakridge said transportation expenses rose to 14 percent of sales from 9 percent. Gross margin declined to 33.1 percent from 35.2 percent, a drop of about two points, which the chief financial officer attributed entirely to shipping costs and storage charges. Delays also left shelves empty in about one in ten of the company's stores during the key back-to-school period.

Management said it has chartered additional vessels, moved part of its volume to a less crowded port on the west coast and begun building inventory earlier in the year. It expects shipping conditions to remain difficult until at least the middle of next year and has not changed its full-year sales forecast. The board maintained the quarterly dividend.
