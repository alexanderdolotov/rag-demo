Oakridge Furnishings reported third-quarter results adversely affected by port congestion and elevated ocean freight rates. Net revenue of USD 1.4 billion rose 3 percent, while net income declined 18 percent to USD 96 million. The company sources the majority of its upholstered furniture, tables and lighting from overseas manufacturers; average container dwell time at discharge ports reached eleven days versus three days in the prior-year period.

Per-container freight expense more than doubled year over year, and logistics costs increased to 14 percent of net revenue from 9 percent. The gross profit ratio contracted to 33.1 percent from 35.2 percent, approximately two percentage points, which the Chief Financial Officer attributed entirely to carriage and storage charges. Delays resulted in stock-outs at approximately one in ten stores during the back-to-school peak.

Mitigating actions include chartering of additional vessels, diversion of a portion of volume to a less congested west coast port and earlier inventory build. Management anticipates constrained logistics conditions through at least mid-next year and left the full-year revenue outlook unchanged. The quarterly dividend was maintained.
