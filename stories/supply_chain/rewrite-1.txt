Oakridge Furnishings said on Wednesday that jammed ports and sky-high shipping prices cut into its profit in the third quarter. Revenue was $1.4 billion, up 3 percent, but net profit dropped 18 percent to $96 million. The company brings most of its sofas, tables and lamps from factories abroad, and said its containers waited an average of eleven days to be unloaded, against three days a year earlier.

The cost of sending a container by sea more than doubled in a year, and Oakridge said freight bills rose to 14 percent of sales from 9 percent. The share of sales left after product costs slipped to 33.1 percent from 35.2 percent, about two points, which the finance chief blamed wholly on transport and warehouse fees. The hold-ups also left bare shelves in roughly one in ten stores during the busy back-to-school weeks.

Executives said they have rented more ships, sent some goods through a quieter port on the west coast and started stocking up earlier. They expect shipping to stay difficult until at least the middle of next year and left their yearly sales forecast alone. The dividend was kept.
