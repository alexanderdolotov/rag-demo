Willowbrook Home Group lowered its full-year outlook on Friday, saying that weak consumer demand had continued into the second half of the year. The company now expects net sales to decline between 4 percent and 6 percent, compared with its earlier forecast of flat to growth of 2 percent, and adjusted earnings per share of $2.10 to $2.30, down from a prior range of $2.80 to $3.00.

Sales at stores open at least a year fell 5.8 percent in the most recent quarter, with the weakest results in furniture and kitchenware, categories that depend on housing activity. Customers bought fewer large items and favored lower-priced alternatives. Traffic in the stores fell 7 percent. The chief executive said shoppers are focused on essentials and are waiting for better value before making bigger purchases, and that the company had not seen signs of a recovery in the first weeks of the current quarter.

Willowbrook said it would cut planned capital spending by $120 million and close twelve underperforming stores by the end of the year, with the loss of about 900 jobs. Shares fell 9 percent in morning trading to their lowest price in three years. The company did not change its dividend.
