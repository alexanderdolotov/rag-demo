Willowbrook Home Group cut its forecast for the year on Friday, saying that shoppers' weak spending had carried on into the second half. The company now predicts that sales will shrink by 4 to 6 percent, rather than the flat to 2 percent growth it said earlier, and that profit per share adjusted for one-offs will be $2.10 to $2.30, compared with $2.80 to $3.00 before.

Sales at stores open for over a year dropped 5.8 percent last quarter, with the biggest declines in furniture and kitchen goods, which rely on home buying and building. People bought fewer big items and chose cheaper options. The number of visitors to stores fell 7 percent. The chief executive said customers are concentrating on necessities and holding off on larger purchases until they see better prices, and that the first weeks of the new quarter showed no rebound.

Willowbrook will reduce planned investment by $120 million and shut twelve weak stores by year end, which will cost about 900 jobs. The stock slid 9 percent in morning trading to a three-year low. The dividend stays the same.
