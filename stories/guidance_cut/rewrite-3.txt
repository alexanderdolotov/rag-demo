I will not dress this up: we are lowering our outlook. People are still not spending, and that carried on into the second half of the year, so we now expect sales to be down four to six percent, when we told you flat to up two. Earnings per share, on our adjusted measure, should be 2.10 to 2.30 dollars, not the 2.80 to 3.00 we promised.

Last quarter, same-store sales fell 5.8 percent, and furniture and kitchenware were worst, because they follow the housing market. Customers bought fewer big things and went for cheaper ones. Foot traffic was down seven percent. When I talk to shoppers they say they will only buy what they need and wait for better deals. The first weeks of this quarter look no different.

So we are cutting spending on new projects by 120 million dollars and closing twelve stores by the end of the year. About 900 people will lose their jobs, and I take that seriously. The stock dropped nine percent this morning to its lowest in three years. The dividend stays as it is.
