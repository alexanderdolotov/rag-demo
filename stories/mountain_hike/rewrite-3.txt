We set off in the dark, four of us with head torches, on the old route that Tomas has been climbing every September for thirty years. He said the view from the top was the best in the valley, and nobody argued. The first part went through spruce woods, up and up in zigzags, over slippery roots and gravel that slid under our boots.

By nine the trees were gone and it was all rock, like steps for giants. It got cold and the air felt thin. We kept stopping for water and chocolate. Then a cloud sat on us for ten minutes and I could not see my own hands, but when it lifted the whole valley was there, with the river like a silver thread.

Around noon we got to a little stone shelter just below the summit. It had a grass roof, a thick wooden door and inside a stove, a bench and a book of names going back decades. We sat for an hour with soup from the flask, listening to the wind. Tomas wrote all four of our names, and then we dragged ourselves over the last ridge.
