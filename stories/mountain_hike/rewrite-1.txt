They climbed the steep hill path before sunrise, with head torches lighting the rocks at their feet. There were four walkers: two sisters, a cousin and an old family friend, Tomas, who had made the walk every September for thirty years and had promised the others the finest view in the valley. The path began in a spruce wood and zigzagged upward over loose gravel and slippery roots.

By nine the trees thinned and the track became a rocky staircase. The air turned cold and thin, and they paused often to sip water and share chocolate. A cloud rolled over the slope and for ten minutes they saw only grey, until it lifted and showed the entire valley below, the river glinting like a thread.

At midday, weary and hungry, they arrived at a small stone cabin close to the top. It was an old low building with a grass roof and a heavy wooden door, kept for walkers by the mountain club. Inside were a little iron stove, a bench and a book where visitors had signed their names for decades. They sheltered there for an hour, drank soup from a flask and listened to the wind. Tomas signed four names, and then they pushed on over the final ridge.
