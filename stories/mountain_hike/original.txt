They hiked up the steep mountain trail before dawn, with headlamps lighting the stones at their feet. There were four of them: two sisters, a cousin and an old family friend, Tomas, who had made the climb every September for thirty years and had promised the others the best view in the valley. The trail began in a forest of spruce and zigzagged upward through patches of loose gravel and wet roots.

By nine o'clock the trees thinned out and the path turned into a rocky staircase. The air grew cold and thin, and they stopped often to drink water and share chocolate. A cloud drifted across the slope and for ten minutes they could see nothing but grey, until it lifted and revealed the whole valley below them, the river shining like a thread.

At noon, tired and hungry, they reached a stone hut near the summit. It was an old, low building with a turf roof and a heavy wooden door, kept for climbers by the mountain club. Inside there was a small iron stove, a wooden bench and a logbook where visitors had written their names for decades. They rested there for an hour, ate soup from a thermos and listened to the wind. Tomas wrote four names in the logbook, and then they pushed on over the final ridge to the summit.
