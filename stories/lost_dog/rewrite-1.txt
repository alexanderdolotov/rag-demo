A missing pet roamed the streets for most of a wet Sunday. The brown animal, of medium size with a white patch on its chest and a worn red collar, had squeezed through a gap in the fence while its owner, Mr. Okafor, was bringing shopping in from the car. By the time he looked up, the gate was swinging and the animal had vanished.

It trotted slowly along the soaked roads, sniffing lampposts and front steps and lapping from a puddle. It went past the bakery, the shuttered school and the corner shop with the striped awning. People glanced at it, but none stopped, assuming it belonged to somebody close by. Twice it flopped down under a bus shelter, cold and hungry, and gazed up the road as though waiting for a familiar whistle.

Late in the afternoon a girl named Mira, heading home from a piano lesson, spotted the shivering creature behind a hedge. She checked the tag on its collar, which carried a phone number, then crouched and spoke gently until it came over. She took it to her mother, who rang the number. Within fifteen minutes Mr. Okafor turned up in his coat and the animal flung itself at his legs. Mira handed the pet back with a proud, serious expression.
