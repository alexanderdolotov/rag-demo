Domestic baking, undertaken on a Saturday morning by an inexperienced cook, followed the traditional sequence. Flour, water, yeast and a small quantity of salt were combined in a large bowl until a cohesive, tacky mass formed, which was then transferred to a floured work surface.

Mechanical development of the gluten required approximately ten minutes of manipulation, involving repeated pushing, folding and quarter turns. The mass initially adhered to the hands but progressively became smooth and extensible, as confirmed by the recovery of an indentation. It was formed into a sphere and allowed to ferment, covered, for one hour in a warm location adjacent to the stove.

On inspection the volume had doubled. The mass was divided, moulded into two oblong units, scored and loaded into a preheated oven. After forty minutes two browned loaves with a crisp crust and a hollow resonance on percussion were removed and left to cool on a wire rack.
