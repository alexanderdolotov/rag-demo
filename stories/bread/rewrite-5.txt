Two crusty brown loaves were cooling on a rack in Ana's kitchen by midday on Saturday, the first she had ever made, and one of them was already missing its end.

She had followed what she remembered of her grandfather's method. A sticky blend of flour, water, yeast and salt was turned out onto a floured table and worked by hand for ten minutes until it turned smooth and bounced back when poked. She put it in the bowl under a damp towel beside the warm stove, and an hour later it had grown to twice its size.

Ana cut it in two, shaped a pair of ovals, slashed the tops and baked them in a hot oven for forty minutes. The loaves sounded hollow when she tapped the bases, which is how you know they are done, and the kitchen smelled of fresh baking.
