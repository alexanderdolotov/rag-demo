I had never baked anything before, but on Saturday I woke up and decided that today was the day. I had watched my grandfather make it so many times that I knew the order by heart. I stirred flour, water, yeast and a pinch of salt in my big blue bowl until it was a sticky lump, and tipped the whole thing out onto the floured table.

Then I worked it with my hands. Ten minutes of pushing and folding and turning. At the start it stuck to everything, and I nearly gave up, but slowly it went smooth and springy, and when I poked it the dent bounced back. I put it back in the bowl, covered it, and let it sit by the stove for an hour.

When I lifted the towel it had doubled, which felt like magic. I cut it into two, shaped the pieces, scored the tops and put them in the oven for forty minutes. The whole flat smelled amazing. They came out golden and crusty, and when I tapped the bottoms they sounded hollow, just like my grandfather said they should. I tore the end off one straight away, with butter.
