I own a few hundred shares of Corvane, so Tuesday's news caught my eye. The company's directors have said it can spend up to five billion dollars buying its own stock over the next three years. That replaces a three billion plan that wrapped up in March, when they snapped up 41 million shares at around 73 dollars each.

They will do it by buying on the open market, and through special arrangements with banks, depending on what the market is doing. The money comes from the cash they have, which was 9.4 billion at the end of the last quarter, plus what the business earns. They have no debt due until 2029, so I am not worried about that part. Nothing forces them to finish the plan, and they can stop whenever they like.

The finance chief called it a sign of confidence and a way of returning spare cash to owners like me. The dividend stays at 52 cents. If they use all of it, about six percent of the shares disappear. The price was up two percent after the close.
