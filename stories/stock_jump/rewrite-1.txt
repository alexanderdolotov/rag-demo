The retailer's stock surged twelve percent on Thursday morning following a strong earnings report, and analysts lifted their targets. Brightmart said it made $1.84 a share for the quarter, far ahead of the $1.52 Wall Street had predicted, with sales of $18.7 billion, nine percent higher than last year.

Sales at stores open at least a year grew 5.1 percent, their best showing in two years, helped by groceries, household goods and online orders picked up in store. Executives said customers were buying pricier items in several areas and that shelves were well stocked for the holidays. They also nudged up their yearly profit forecast to between $7.10 and $7.30 a share.

Brokerages moved fast. Three raised their goals by $10 to $15 a share, and another changed its advice from hold to buy, arguing that Brightmart is winning customers from weaker competitors. The stock ended the day at $96.40, the highest since spring, on trading four times heavier than normal. A few investors warned that the climb had begun before the report and that weaker consumer spending might return.
