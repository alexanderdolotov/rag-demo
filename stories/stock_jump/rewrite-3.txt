I woke up Thursday to find my Brightmart position up 12 percent before I had finished my coffee. The company had reported earnings of $1.84 a share when everyone expected $1.52, with sales of $18.7 billion, up 9 percent. I did not see that coming.

What impressed me was that the growth was broad. Same-store sales rose 5.1 percent, the best in two years, with groceries, home goods and order-online-pick-up-in-store all doing well. The management team said people were buying more expensive things and that they were not stuck with extra inventory going into the holidays. They also raised their earnings outlook for the year to $7.10 to $7.30.

By lunchtime the analysts were falling over each other. Three raised their targets by $10 to $15, and one switched from hold to buy. The shares closed at $96.40, the highest since spring, on massive volume. A friend of mine thinks it has run too far already, and I can see his point, because spending could cool off again.
