Brightmart finished Thursday at $96.40 a share, its highest close since spring and 12 percent higher on the day, after a quarterly report that left Wall Street's forecasts behind.

The retailer earned $1.84 per share against an expected $1.52, on revenue of $18.7 billion, up 9 percent. Sales at established stores grew 5.1 percent, the best in two years, boosted by food, household items and online orders collected at the store. Management said customers were buying higher-priced goods, said inventory was in good shape for the holidays and raised its annual outlook to $7.10 to $7.30 per share.

Analysts followed with higher valuations: three raised their targets by $10 to $15 and one moved from hold to buy. Trading volume was four times normal. Some investors cautioned that the shares had climbed before the report and that shoppers could pull back.
