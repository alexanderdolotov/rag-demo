Lakemont Logistics disclosed in a regulatory filing on Monday that it had experienced a cybersecurity incident. The company detected unauthorized access to part of its corporate network on May 6, and immediately took affected systems offline to contain the activity. Its freight scheduling and warehouse management platforms were unavailable for four days, and about 11 percent of shipments were delayed during that period.

Lakemont said it has engaged an outside forensic firm and notified federal law enforcement. Based on the investigation to date, the company has found no evidence that customer payment information or employee records were accessed, although the review is continuing. The incident is not expected to affect the company's ability to meet its obligations under existing contracts.

The company restored full operations on May 10 and has reset credentials across its network and added further monitoring of its systems. Lakemont estimated that the incident would cost between $15 million and $20 million in the second quarter, including investigation fees and lost revenue, and that most of this would be recovered under its cybersecurity insurance policy. The chief executive apologized to customers whose deliveries were late and said the company would offer service credits to those affected.
