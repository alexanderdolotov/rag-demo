A toad rested on the soggy shore of the stream while the current climbed. Three days of rain in the hills had turned what was a slow, brown trickle all summer into a loud, fast flood. Twigs and tufts of grass spun by on the current, and the noise of moving water filled the valley.

The toad had lived at this spot since its days as a tadpole and knew every root and pebble. Now it watched the water slide over the lowest roots, then the flat rock where it liked to bask, then the first grassy mounds. Every time the stream swallowed another landmark the toad edged higher up the shore, pressing its toes into the cold, wet earth.

A group of ducks paddled quickly away downstream. A dead limb rolled along the bottom and snagged on an alder. The air smelled of damp soil and bruised leaves. The toad was watchful but calm; water was its home, and it had seen floods before. Late in the afternoon the rain lightened and the stream stopped rising. At dusk the level started to sink, exposing the flat rock bit by bit. The toad stayed until dark and then crept back down the slick shore.
