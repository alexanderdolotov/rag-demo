The frog sat on the muddy bank of the river and watched the water rise. It had rained for three days in the hills, and the river that had been a quiet brown ribbon all summer was now swollen and fast. Branches and clumps of grass floated past, turning slowly, and the roar of the water filled the whole valley.

The frog had lived on this bank since it was a tadpole and knew every root and stone along the slope. Now it saw the water creeping over the lowest roots, then the flat rock where it liked to sun itself, then the first of the grassy hummocks. Each time the river covered another familiar landmark the frog moved a little higher up, sinking its toes into the cold mud.

A family of ducks hurried downstream. A dead branch scraped along the bottom and lodged against the alder. The air smelled of wet earth and crushed leaves. The frog stayed alert but unafraid; water was its element, and it had seen the river rise before.

By late afternoon the rain eased. The frog was perched among the alder roots, halfway up the bank, and the river had stopped climbing. For an hour it sat watching the brown water slide by. When evening came the level began to fall by a finger's width, then another, uncovering the flat rock one slow hand at a time. The frog sat on until dark, then slid down into the mud again.
