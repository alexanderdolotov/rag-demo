An amphibian occupied the saturated embankment of the watercourse during a period of rising discharge. Three consecutive days of precipitation in the upland catchment had converted a low-flow summer channel into a turbid, high-velocity flood. Debris, including branches and vegetation mats, was conveyed downstream, and the sound of the flow was audible throughout the valley.

The individual had resided at this location since the larval stage and was familiar with its microtopography. Inundation progressively covered the lowest roots, the basking rock and the initial vegetated mounds. In response to each submerged landmark the amphibian ascended the slope in small increments, anchoring its digits in cold sediment.

Waterfowl moved rapidly downstream. A fallen limb dragged along the channel bed and lodged against an alder. The animal remained vigilant but unalarmed, being adapted to aquatic conditions. By late afternoon precipitation diminished and the water level ceased to rise. After approximately one hour the stage began to recede, exposing the basking rock progressively. The amphibian remained until nightfall before descending the wet embankment.
