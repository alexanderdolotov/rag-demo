Three days of rain up in the hills, and by the time I came down to my spot the river had changed completely. All summer it had been a lazy brown ribbon. Now it was swollen and loud, dragging sticks and clumps of grass along, and I could feel the roar in my feet.

I have been on this bank since I was a tadpole, so I know every stone. I saw the water climb over the low roots, then the flat rock where I like to warm up, then the grassy lumps. Each time it took something I knew, I shuffled further up the slope and dug my toes into the cold mud.

Ducks went by in a hurry. A dead branch scraped along the bottom and jammed against the alder. I was not frightened. Water is where I belong, and I have seen the river rise before. By late afternoon the rain was easing and the river had stopped coming. I sat there an hour just watching it slide past, and when evening came it began to drop, bit by bit, giving my flat rock back. I stayed till dark.
