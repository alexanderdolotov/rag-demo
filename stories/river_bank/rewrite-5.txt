By evening the flood had crested and the stream began to fall, leaving a toad safe among the alder roots halfway up the shore where it had retreated during the day.

The trouble began after three days of rain in the hills, when the normally quiet brown stream became a swift, noisy torrent carrying branches and grass. The toad, which had lived on this shore since it was a tadpole, watched the current climb over the low roots, the sunning rock and the first grassy mounds, and moved up the slope each time a landmark disappeared.

Ducks fled downstream and a dead limb jammed against an alder. The toad stayed alert but calm, since it had seen the stream rise before. By late afternoon the rain eased and the water stopped climbing. An hour later the level began to drop, exposing the rock again slowly, and the toad lingered until dark before sliding back down the wet shore.
