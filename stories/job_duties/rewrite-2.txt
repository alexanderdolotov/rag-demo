Position: night security attendant, Aldous Museum. The incumbent, Walter Pruitt, had served for twenty-two years and could recite the full scope of his responsibilities from memory. At 22:00 he secured the main entrance and the side door. From 23:00 he conducted the first patrol, covering all galleries across three floors with a torch and verifying that each window was latched. At each hour he recorded the time and the entry 'clear' in the incident register, in accordance with procedure.

Ancillary tasks were less formal. He monitored temperature and humidity instruments in the paintings galleries, since aged canvases are prone to cracking in dry air; wound the entrance hall clock weekly on Thursdays; and inspected the basement boiler at 02:00. Watering the ferns in the atrium was not a prescribed task but was tacitly valued by the director.

On a March night, during the 03:00 patrol, the attendant detected a cold draught in the east gallery and found a small window above the staircase open. No loss or intruder was identified. The window was secured, the time and location logged, and the director informed by telephone. In the attendant's words, the work consists chiefly of observation and of noticing deviations from the previous day.
