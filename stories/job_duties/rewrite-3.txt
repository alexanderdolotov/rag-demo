People ask me what a night watchman actually does, and I tell them it is mostly walking and looking. I have worked at the Aldous Museum for twenty-two years, and I could do my job with my eyes shut. At ten I lock the front and side doors. From eleven I go round all three floors with my big torch, checking every window is latched, and each hour I write the time and the word clear in the book, because that is the rule.

There are other things the job needs that are not written down. I check the thermometer and the humidity dial in the painting rooms, because the old canvases crack when the air is dry. I wind the clock in the hall on Thursdays. At two I go down to the cellar and look at the boiler. And I water the ferns, which nobody asked me to do but I think the director likes it.

One March night at three I felt a draught in the east gallery and found a little window open above the stairs. Nothing was gone and nobody was there. I shut it, wrote it down and rang the director at home. The thing about this job is noticing what is different from yesterday.
