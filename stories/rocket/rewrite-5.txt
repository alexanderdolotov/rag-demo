A new storm-tracking satellite is circling the Earth tonight, 800 kilometres up, after the vehicle carrying it reached space in under ten minutes from a coastal pad.

The launch took place at midday after two delays caused by wind and cloud. Thousands watched from the beach as the white booster climbed on a plume of fire; mission control reported ignition, peak aerodynamic loading and first-stage cutoff in turn. The second stage then burned cleanly, the nose cone fell into the sea, and the craft was released into a circular path.

Within twenty minutes the satellite opened its solar panels and sent its first signal. It is the first of a series meant to follow storms and measure sea temperatures, with usable data due in about six weeks and hurricane warnings expected to improve by several hours.
