The launch vehicle lifted off from the coastal range at 12:00 local time under clear, windy conditions. Spectators positioned several kilometres away observed the white booster ascend on a column of exhaust, accelerating gradually until the acoustic signature arrived as a prolonged crackle. The launch control centre announced each event in sequence: ignition, maximum dynamic pressure and first-stage engine cutoff.

Orbital insertion was achieved in under ten minutes. The second stage burned nominally, the payload shroud was jettisoned into the ocean, and the vehicle entered a circular orbit at an altitude of 800 kilometres. Operations staff expressed relief following extensive qualification testing and two weather-related scrubs.

The payload was an environmental monitoring satellite, the first of a planned constellation intended to observe tropical cyclones and measure sea surface temperature. Following separation, solar arrays deployed and a first telemetry signal was received at the ground station within twenty minutes. Commissioning of the instruments will take approximately six weeks. Meteorological agencies anticipate improved cyclone warning lead times of several hours.
