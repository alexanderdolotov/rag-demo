We got to the beach at ten and waited two hours in the wind. At noon exactly a tiny bright point appeared above the horizon and I realised the launch vehicle was rising, silent at first because of the distance, and then the sound hit us like a wave and everyone around me started to shout.

On the big screen they counted off every step, the engines, the moment of maximum pressure, the first stage shutting down. The white machine got smaller and smaller until I lost it in the haze. About nine minutes later the commentator said it had made it into space and the crowd cheered.

My daughter asked what it was carrying. I told her a climate observation craft, the first of a whole series for following storms and measuring how warm the sea is. They said it would unfold its panels and call home within twenty minutes, and that the data would start in about six weeks. If it means the hurricane warnings come hours earlier, that is good enough for me.
