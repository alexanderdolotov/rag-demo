The creature survived the day because it never once moved. By nightfall it was still hidden among the reeds, unharmed, after spending twelve hours in the same spot while a grey wading bird hunted a few paces away.

The ordeal began at dawn, when the small green animal woke in its usual place at the shallow end of the pond and prepared for its normal routine of calling, sunning on a flat stone and swimming to the lily pads. It then spotted the predator, standing with one leg raised and its neck coiled, eyes locked on the water, and understood the danger.

It held perfectly still, belly pressed into the mud, colouring blending into the vegetation. The sun climbed, flies crawled over it, and once the large bird came a step closer; its heart pounded but its body did not stir. In the afternoon the bird speared a fish and resumed watching. The animal stayed frozen until dusk, when the bird finally flew away, and then waited in the dark before slipping out of hiding.
