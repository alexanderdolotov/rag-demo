The amphibian exhibited complete immobility throughout the morning. Having emerged before sunrise in its customary position within the emergent vegetation at the shallow margin of the pond, it had intended to follow its established routine: brief vocalisation, a bound to the exposed flat stone for thermoregulation, and a swim to the lily pads where prey insects aggregate.

Upon opening its eyes fully, the individual detected a predator. A large grey heron was standing in the shallows at a distance of approximately ten paces, motionless, with one leg raised and the neck folded. The predator's gaze was directed at the water surface. The amphibian concluded that any abrupt movement would provoke an attack.

It therefore remained still, pressing its ventral surface into the cool sediment while its cryptic dorsal colouring merged with the vegetation. Several hours elapsed. Ambient temperature rose as the mist dispersed. Flies landed on its head without response. When the heron advanced a single step, cardiac rate increased markedly, yet no muscular movement occurred. Only at dusk, after the predator departed on heavy wingbeats, did respiration normalise, and immobility continued until full darkness.
