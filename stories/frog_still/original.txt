The frog did not jump that morning. It had woken before the sun in its usual place among the reeds at the shallow end of the pond, ready for the usual routine: a few quiet calls, a leap to the flat stone to dry its skin, a long swim across to the lily pads where the flies gathered.

But when it opened its eyes fully, it saw the heron. The tall grey heron stood in the water not ten steps away, perfectly still, one leg lifted, its long neck folded into an S. Its yellow eye was fixed on the water, and the frog understood at once that any sudden movement would end badly.

So the frog stayed motionless. It pressed its belly into the cool mud and let its green-brown back blend with the reeds. Hours went by. The sun climbed, the mist burned off, and the heat gathered over the pond. Flies buzzed around its face and it did not flick them away. Once the heron took a slow step forward and the frog felt its own heart hammering, yet nothing in its body moved.

In the afternoon the heron stabbed at the water and came up with a small silver fish, swallowed it, and went back to waiting. Only when the light turned orange and faded, and the heron at last lifted its heavy wings and flapped away over the trees, did the frog let out its breath. It stayed in the reeds until it was completely dark, and only then, with a soft splash, did it finally move.
