I did not move a muscle that morning, and I will tell you why. I had woken early in my spot in the reeds, the way I always do, planning my usual day: a few calls, a hop up to my flat stone to dry off, and a swim over to the lily pads where the flies hang about.

Then I opened my eyes properly and there it was, a huge grey bird, standing in the water right beside me. One leg tucked up, neck bent like a hook. That yellow eye was fixed on the water and I knew what it meant. One twitch and I would be lunch.

So I stayed exactly where I was. I pushed my belly down into the mud and let myself look like part of the reeds. The hours crawled. The sun came up, the mist burned off, it got hot. Flies walked across my face and I let them. When the bird took a step toward me my heart nearly burst, but I held still. It caught a little fish in the afternoon, ate it, and went back to watching. Only when the light turned orange and the bird flapped off did I breathe again, and even then I waited for full dark.
