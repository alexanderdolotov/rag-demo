Verita Systems said on Monday that it has settled a patent infringement lawsuit brought by Ardent Networks, ending a legal fight that began four years ago. Under the settlement, Verita will pay Ardent $200 million, half of it now and the rest in four equal quarterly installments. The companies also agreed to a cross-license of the disputed patents, which cover methods for routing data between wireless devices.

Ardent had sued in a federal court in Texas, claiming that Verita's latest routers used its patented technology without permission. A jury found in Ardent's favor last year and awarded $310 million, but Verita had appealed the verdict, and a ruling was not expected for at least another year. Verita denied any wrongdoing throughout the case, and the settlement states that it admits no liability.

Verita said it will record a charge of $200 million in the current quarter, which will reduce earnings per share by about 31 cents, and that the settlement removes uncertainty about future sales of its routers. The general counsel said the board concluded that settling was in the best interest of shareholders because it avoids further legal costs of about $25 million a year. Ardent said it was pleased to resolve the dispute, and Verita will withdraw its appeal.
