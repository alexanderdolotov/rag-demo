Verita Systems said on Monday that it has ended a legal dispute over patents with Ardent Networks that started four years ago. Verita agreed to pay Ardent $200 million, half now and the rest in four equal payments every three months. The two companies also agreed to let each other use the patents in question, which cover ways of directing data between wireless gadgets.

Ardent had taken Verita to a federal court in Texas, arguing that the newest Verita routers used its technology without permission. A jury sided with Ardent last year and ordered $310 million in damages, but Verita had challenged the decision and no ruling was expected for at least another year. Verita denied doing anything wrong all along, and the agreement says it accepts no blame.

Verita will take a $200 million charge this quarter, cutting profit per share by about 31 cents, and said the deal removes doubt about future sales of its routers. The company's top lawyer said the board decided that ending the fight was best for owners because it avoids about $25 million a year in legal bills. Ardent said it was pleased, and Verita will drop its challenge.
