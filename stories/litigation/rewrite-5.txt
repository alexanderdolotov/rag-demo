Verita Systems will pay $200 million to end a four-year patent fight with Ardent Networks, avoiding a possible year-long appeal after a jury had ordered it to pay $310 million.

Half of the money is due now and the rest in four equal quarterly instalments. The companies also agreed to license the disputed patents, which cover how data is routed between wireless devices, to each other. Verita says it did nothing wrong and the agreement contains no admission of liability; it will drop its appeal.

Ardent had sued in federal court in Texas, claiming Verita's latest routers used its technology without permission. Verita will take a $200 million charge this quarter, trimming earnings per share by about 31 cents, but its general counsel said the board chose to end the case to remove uncertainty over router sales and to save about $25 million a year in legal costs.
