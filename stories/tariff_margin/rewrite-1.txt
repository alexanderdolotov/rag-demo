Northvale Devices announced results for its third fiscal quarter, which ended June 27. Revenue came to $41.2 billion, 6 percent more than a year ago, driven by high demand for the newest phones and a record quarter for subscription services. Operating profit climbed to $12.6 billion.

Profit margins benefited by roughly two percentage points because the company recovered import duties it had paid in earlier periods, after a court struck down part of the charges. The overall share of each sales dollar left after product costs was 50.1 percent, up from 46.3 percent a year earlier. Without the recovered money it would have been about 48.1 percent, which was still better than last year thanks to a stronger mix of products and cheaper parts.

The finance chief warned investors not to expect the same windfall again. The recovered duties were booked as a cut to the cost of goods in the quarter they came in, and the company's forecast assumes no more. For the next quarter management predicts margins of 47 to 48 percent. Directors approved a dividend of 26 cents a share, to be paid next month to holders on record by August 11.
