Halden Appliance Corporation reported fiscal second quarter results that fell short of its own forecast, and management put most of the blame on new tariffs. Net sales were $3.1 billion, flat compared with last year. Net income fell to $214 million from $297 million.

New tariffs on imported components raised our costs during the quarter, said the chief executive in the earnings release. The company buys motors, circuit boards and compressors from suppliers in Asia, and the additional tariffs of 25 percent that took effect in March applied to nearly all of them. Gross margin was 27.4 percent, down from 30.5 percent a year earlier, a squeeze of about three points that the company attributes almost entirely to the tariffs.

Halden said it raised list prices on most products by 4 percent in May, but retailers absorbed only part of the increase and unit volumes declined in the last six weeks of the quarter. The company is qualifying new suppliers in Mexico and Vietnam, a process management expects to take until next spring. Until then it expects the tariff cost to remain a drag on margin of roughly the same size. The board kept the quarterly dividend unchanged at $0.40 per share.
