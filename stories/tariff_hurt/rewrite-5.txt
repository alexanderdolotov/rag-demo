Halden Appliance cut into its own earnings this quarter: profit fell by more than a quarter to $214 million, and the company said a surge in what it pays to bring in foreign components was behind most of the drop.

The cause was an extra 25 percent charge on imported motors, circuit boards and compressors that began in March and covers almost everything Halden buys in Asia. It lowered the proportion of each sales dollar left after production costs to 27.4 percent, from 30.5 a year before, roughly a three-point squeeze.

Halden raised prices by 4 percent in May but shoppers and retailers took only part of it, and units sold fell in the final six weeks. Sales stayed at $3.1 billion. Alternative suppliers in Mexico and Vietnam should be approved by next spring; until then the same pressure on profitability is expected. The dividend remains 40 cents.
