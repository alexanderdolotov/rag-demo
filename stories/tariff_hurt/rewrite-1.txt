Halden Appliance Corporation said its second-quarter results missed the company's own forecast, and executives pointed mainly at higher import duties. Revenue was $3.1 billion, unchanged from a year ago, while profit dropped to $214 million from $297 million.

Higher import duties on parts increased expenses during the period, the chief executive said in the announcement. Halden buys motors, circuit boards and compressors from factories in Asia, and an extra 25 percent charge that began in March hit almost all of those items. The share of sales left after product costs fell to 27.4 percent from 30.5 percent, a drop of about three points that the company blames almost entirely on the new charges.

To cope, Halden lifted sticker prices on most products by 4 percent in May, but stores passed along only part of the increase and the number of units sold fell in the last six weeks. New suppliers in Mexico and Vietnam are being approved, which management says will take until next spring, and until then the extra cost will keep weighing on profitability by a similar amount. The dividend stays at 40 cents.
