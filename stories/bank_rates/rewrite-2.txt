The Monetary Policy Committee increased the policy rate by 25 basis points on Wednesday, the fifth tightening in twelve months, with the objective of reducing inflation that remains materially above target. The rate stands at 4.75 percent, the highest since 2007. The decision was adopted by eight votes to one, the dissenting member favouring a hold.

The accompanying statement noted that annual consumer price inflation of 5.4 percent remained excessive and that labour market conditions were tight, with unemployment near a fifty-year low and nominal wage growth above 5 percent sustaining services inflation. The Governor indicated that further tightening would be undertaken as required, contingent on incoming data.

Retail lenders adjusted mortgage pricing upward within hours; estimates suggest that holders of variable-rate loans will incur approximately USD 60 in additional monthly payments. Sovereign yields rose and the exchange rate appreciated against the dollar. Market participants anticipate one further increase prior to a pause, with easing unlikely before next year.
