The monetary authority lifted its benchmark rate by twenty-five basis points on Wednesday, the fifth rise in a year, to cool rising prices that are still far above where it wants them. The rate is now 4.75 percent, the highest since 2007. Eight of the nine policymakers backed the move, and one wanted to wait.

In its statement the authority said consumer prices were up 5.4 percent over the past year, which is too fast, and that jobs are still plentiful, with unemployment close to a fifty-year low. Pay rises of over 5 percent have kept the cost of services climbing. At a news conference the governor said borrowing costs would keep going up for as long as necessary, though each step would depend on the latest numbers.

Firms that offer home loans raised their prices within hours, and analysts calculated that households on floating-rate mortgages will pay roughly $60 more a month. Government bond yields rose and the currency gained on the dollar. Economists predict one more increase before a pause, and no reduction until next year.
