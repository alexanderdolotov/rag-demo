Borrowers will pay more from today: the nation's monetary authority raised its policy rate to 4.75 percent, the highest since 2007, in a bid to push price growth back toward its goal.

The quarter-point move, the fifth in twelve months, passed eight votes to one. Prices rose 5.4 percent over the past year, and with unemployment near a half-century low and pay growth above 5 percent, the committee judged that services costs would keep rising without further action. The governor promised more increases if needed, but said they would depend on data.

Home loan providers raised their prices within hours, adding about $60 a month for a typical variable mortgage. Yields on government bonds rose and the currency gained on the dollar. Most economists foresee one more rise before the pause, with a cut unlikely this year.
