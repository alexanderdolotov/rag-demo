The central bank raised interest rates by a quarter point on Wednesday, its fifth increase in twelve months, in an effort to slow inflation that has stayed well above its target. The policy rate now stands at 4.75 percent, the highest level since 2007. The decision was approved by eight of the nine members of the rate-setting committee, with one member voting for a pause.

In a statement the bank said that inflation, which was 5.4 percent in the latest annual reading, remains too high, and that the labour market is still tight, with unemployment near a fifty-year low. Wage growth of more than 5 percent has kept price pressures in services elevated. The governor said at a press conference that the committee would keep raising rates for as long as needed, but that future decisions would depend on the data.

Mortgage lenders responded within hours by raising the rates they offer to home buyers, and analysts said households with variable-rate loans will see their monthly payments rise by around $60 on a typical mortgage. Bond yields climbed and the currency strengthened against the dollar. Economists expect at least one more increase before the bank pauses, with a cut not likely before next year.
