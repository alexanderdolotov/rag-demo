Harbor Trust Bancorp reported second-quarter results on Thursday, saying that it increased its provision for credit losses to $85 million from $32 million a year earlier because of growing stress in its commercial real estate loan portfolio. Net income fell 41 percent to $118 million, or $0.74 per share.

The bank said nonperforming loans rose to 1.9 percent of total loans from 1.1 percent, driven mostly by office buildings in three large cities where vacancy rates have climbed above 20 percent. Net charge-offs, the loans the bank wrote off as uncollectible, were $46 million, double the figure of the previous quarter. The allowance for credit losses now stands at $780 million, or 1.8 percent of loans.

Deposits were stable at $41 billion, and the bank's core capital ratio remained at 11.2 percent, comfortably above regulatory requirements. The chief risk officer told analysts that the bank had reviewed every commercial property loan above $10 million and expected losses to be manageable but to continue for several quarters. Management said it would not seek to grow its commercial real estate book this year and would focus on lending to small businesses. The quarterly dividend of $0.34 per share was maintained.
