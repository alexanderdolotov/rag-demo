Harbor Trust Bancorp reported results for the second quarter on Thursday and said the lender set aside $85 million to cover loans that may go bad, up from $32 million a year ago, because of growing trouble in its loans on commercial property. Profit fell 41 percent to $118 million, or 74 cents a share.

Loans that borrowers have stopped paying rose to 1.9 percent of all loans from 1.1 percent, mostly office buildings in three big cities where more than a fifth of space stands empty. Bad debts written off as uncollectible came to $46 million, twice the previous quarter's amount. The reserve for loan losses now stands at $780 million, or 1.8 percent of lending.

Customer savings held steady at $41 billion, and the main capital cushion stayed at 11.2 percent, well above what regulators demand. The chief risk officer said the lender had re-checked every property loan above $10 million and expected the write-offs to be manageable but to continue for several quarters. Management will not expand its property lending this year and will focus on small business loans. The dividend of 34 cents a share was kept.
