On Thursday's call I tried to be as plain as possible about what we are seeing. Our commercial property loans are under stress, especially office buildings in three big cities, where more than one in five offices is empty. So we put 85 million dollars aside for loans that might not be repaid, compared with 32 million a year ago, and our profit dropped 41 percent to 118 million, or 74 cents a share.

The share of loans where borrowers have stopped paying went up to 1.9 percent from 1.1. We wrote off 46 million dollars of debts we do not expect to collect, double the last quarter, and our reserve is now 780 million, 1.8 percent of everything we have lent.

The good news is that our customers' deposits are stable at 41 billion and our capital is at 11.2 percent, well above what regulators ask. We have gone through every property loan over 10 million dollars. I expect the losses to be manageable but to carry on for several quarters. We will not grow property lending this year, and the dividend stays at 34 cents.
